VIC granny flat approvals in Geelong

In short: In Victoria, a granny flat is a small second dwelling of up to 80 m². In most residential zones it will not need a planning permit if the lot is at least 300 m² and no overlay or other control requires one, but a building permit is still required.

A summary of official rules, with links to the source pages. It is not legal or planning advice.

Last updated: 24 November 2026

This page summarises official Victorian planning information so you know what to expect. It is not legal or planning advice. Your builder, a building surveyor or the local council can confirm what applies to your lot.

What counts as a small second dwelling

Victoria's planning system calls a granny flat a "small second dwelling": a self-contained residence of no more than 80 square metres, on the same lot as an existing dwelling, with a kitchen sink, food preparation facilities, a bath or shower, and a toilet and wash basin. The 80 m² floor-area limit is measured from the outside of the external walls (or the centre of a party wall) and does not include unroofed patios, pergolas, porches or terraces, or roofed decks and verandahs attached to the dwelling. Amendment VC306 raised the previous 60 m² limit to 80 m² on 15 October 2026.

Source: Department of Transport and Planning, Small second homes (page updated 15 October 2026).

When you need a planning permit

A small second dwelling is permitted in all residential and rural zones and normally will not need a planning permit in those zones unless the lot is less than 300 m² (in a residential zone), the land is in a Rural Conservation, Green Wedge or Green Wedge A Zone, a rural zone sets extra permit triggers, or another provision of the planning scheme requires a permit. A planning permit may also be required under an overlay. In a Heritage, Design and Development or Neighbourhood Character Overlay, a small second dwelling does not need a planning permit if the building is no more than 5 metres high and is finished in muted tones and colours. Car parking does not need to be provided for a small second dwelling. A planning permit cannot be granted to create a separate lot for land containing a small second dwelling. Restrictive covenants and section 173 agreements can still limit what you can build, so check the title.

Building permits

A building permit is normally required to develop a small second dwelling, including when a planning permit is also required. Siting and design requirements are usually assessed by the relevant building surveyor as part of the building permit, except on lots under 300 m² in a residential zone where the siting assessment is done by council as part of the planning permit. There are no restrictions on who can live in a small second dwelling, and rental laws apply if you rent it out.

Local council: Geelong

Your local council is City of Greater Geelong. Check the council's planning information for your address (zoning, overlays, bush fire, flood or heritage mapping), as these can change what is allowed for a granny flat. Your builder or certifier normally does this check as part of the quote.

Some Geelong streets are covered by heritage overlays in the planning scheme, and new estates often have their own design guidelines. Check both before you settle on a design.

Builders, contracts and insurance

In Victoria, domestic building work over $10,000 must be done by a registered builder under a written major domestic building contract. For work over $16,000, the builder must give you a domestic building insurance policy and certificate of currency before taking any payment. The deposit can be no more than 10% if the contract price is under $20,000, or 5% if it is $20,000 or more. Victoria's building regulator is now the Building and Plumbing Commission (BPC), which replaced the VBA, and you can check a builder's registration with it.

Sources: Consumer Affairs Victoria, What work requires a building contract; Consumer Affairs Victoria, Deposits and payments; Consumer Affairs Victoria, Domestic building insurance.
